NOTE S-1.  ASSUMPTIONS APPLICABLE TO ALL COLUMNS

SIMPLIFIED · FEDERAL ONLY

(a) Married filing jointly; federal tax only. For 2026, the 37% MFJ bracket begins above $768,700 of taxable income; cumulative federal tax at that threshold is ≈$206,600, rounded in the schedule to ≈$207,000. Top-bracket deductions are quoted at a 37% marginal rate; deductions that burn down into lower brackets are valued through the applicable bracket bands. State tax excluded; in conforming high-tax states, savings increase. Medicare surtaxes and NIIT are unaffected by the deduction. QBI interactions ignored.

(b) Fund profile per reference strategy, benchmarked to public reporting on AQR TA Delphi Plus and used only for Schedule SC-1's simplified year-one economics. Two separate modeling inputs are shown: a 14% annual reference-case gross pre-tax economic return target, and 30% annual ordinary-loss production via notional-principal-contract payment legs. The operating record's Item 5 reports separate YTD attribution. Deferred economic gains are assumed, for illustration, to be realized at the top federal long-term capital gains rate plus NIIT (≈23.8%), excluding state tax; actual production, character, and timing depend on strategy performance, trading path, instrument mix, holding period, fund accounting, and counsel-approved reporting.

(c) Fees: 1.75% of subscription plus 20% of gains over a 4% T-bill hurdle, incentive computed net of the management fee. As trader-fund expenses, fees flow through as additional ordinary deductions.

(d) §461(l): net business losses deductible against non-business income up to $512,000 (MFJ, for tax years beginning in 2026); the excess converts to an indefinite NOL carryforward usable against 80% of taxable income per year and not re-tested under §461(l). Business losses offset the household's business income without limitation before the cap applies.

(e) Sizing rule: subscription = target usable loss ÷ 30%, where the target is floored at income taxed at 32% or above (the burn-down floor) and capped at household headroom.

(f) NPC accrual-match gate: ordinary-loss benefits are shown only if the fund tax package supports matched deduction/accrual treatment, reasonable amortization, §475 mark-to-market, hedge accounting, or another counsel-approved method. Pending or failed support contributes zero to usable household value.

NOTE S-3.  COMMENTARY

READ WITH RECORD ITEMS 2 AND 6

COL. A — THE CORE CLIENT. Right-sized at $1.715M, the household pays $58,000 in fees to save $189,000 in tax in each recurring year, with ≈$182,000 of economic gain compounding toward long-term rates. The intentionally small NOL spillover drains the following year. This is the repeatable subscription: a fixed annual slab of the client's income is sheltered at full marginal rates, every year, with capture near unity.

COL. B — THE FLAGSHIP. Because business losses net against the household's $2.5M of K-1 income before §461(l) applies, $2.67M of deductions land with zero stranding — the bill falls 81%. The binding constraint is portfolio concentration, not tax law. Households of this profile should be sourced deliberately; the register flags them.

COL. C — VIABLE, RARELY OPTIMAL. The burn-down floor caps the budget at ≈$300,000 despite $512,000 of statutory headroom, and the realized rate falls to ≈34%. Per dollar the economics hold, but the absolute benefit is small against the suitability strain of committing $1M to a levered trader fund. The default recommendation for this profile is the Phase A harvesting SMA.

COL. D — WHAT THE THROTTLE PREVENTS. The same client as Column A, sized by comfort instead of headroom: fees exceed the usable year-one benefit, $2.8M of losses queue behind the 80% carryforward grind, and capture falls to 0.19 — flagging the account as primarily tax-motivated. The position is defensible only as a $10M investment judged on its pre-tax merits; as a tax trade it is rejected at the pre-trade gate. This column is retained in the schedule as the standing illustration of why subscriptions are sized by the State Engine and not by appetite.
